## How to use Elder Scrolls as a base (and only as a base)

We borrow a **shape**, not a setting:

- A public temple court of high gods, plus a wilder court of bargains.
- Demigods who were mortal and rose through a deed.
- Long-lived vampire clans with old houses and old grudges.
- Werewolf packs in the wild, with their own honors.
- Mortal institutions (banks, guilds, cities) that claim a god's blessing and fight each other.

We do **not** use their names, places, races, or identities. None of these belong in our data or dialogue: Akatosh, Aedra, Daedra, Nirn, Morrowind, Skyrim, Cyrodiil, the Tribunal, Talos, Hircine, Azura, Volkihar, the famous vampire clans of that series, or its race names. If a generator would emit one, it is rejected (see `latin_names.json`).

## Later Earth, in short

It is Earth, far in the future. Old relics survive, in the spirit of *A Canticle for Leibowitz* (inspiration only; we use our own names and relics). Imperial villages sit near giant mushrooms, erupting volcanoes, and beautiful seas with huge harbors and shipyards. Neon towers stand next to guild halls. Cloning labs sit beside warlock towers. Magic and science both work, and neither one wins.

Peoples already named by Blake live together: humans, Nordics, gray aliens and other aliens, elves, orks, dragons, fairies, demons, liches, vampires, werewolves, and others still using placeholder names (the Krishna-like and Ganesh-like peoples). Dragons and fairies help the people and the monks.

Power today looks like corporations, the heirs of old kingdoms. After the first patriarch or matriarch died, kingdoms rose. Those kingdoms warred with vampires for a very long time. The **Great Wall** was built to keep vampires out. It stops at the mountains, because vampires do not like mountains. Werewolves live in the wilderness, and some packs live in those same mountains.

Three standing rivalries run through the whole bible:

1. Guilds fighting each other, and sometimes themselves.
2. Magic versus science.
3. Vampires versus humans, especially **vampire clans versus banking clans**.

## The Kindled Court (eight high gods)

These are the public temple gods of the later cities. Anyone may leave a gift. Stories about them should be gentle enough for a child, and still have teeth for a grown-up rivalry.

1. **Lumenor** (he/him) — Light, day, and honest work. The starter spell word *lux* is taught in his shrines.
2. **Terrasa** (she/her) — Soil, farms, planting, and harvest. Garden plots are a small nod to her.
3. **Marisca** (she/her) — Seas, harbors, shipwrights, and a calm after a storm. Port Paradise keeps her a dockside shrine.
4. **Ferron** (he/him) — Making: forges, engines, starship plates, and tools. Scientists and smiths both claim him, and they argue about it.
5. **Sapheira** (she/her) — Wisdom, schools, maps, and memory. She is a teacher, not a watcher. No eye symbols in her art; her sign is an open book and a lamp.
6. **Belloran** (he/him) — Just defense, guards, and the Great Wall. He is not a god of conquest stories for kids; he is the one who says "hold the gate."
7. **Numera** (she/her) — Fair counting, contracts, weights in the market, and banks. Human banking clans and some vampire clans both claim her, which is why they fight in her name as much as over coin.
8. **Somnara** (she/her) — Sleep, steerable dreams, and the astral road. This is the in-world reason sleep is fun in the game.

A city temple usually seats all eight. A small shrine may seat one. Faction splits often start when a hall favors one god and ignores another.

## The Dusk Court (four wild powers)

Not a "villain church." These are bargains, night roads, and wild change. Kid mode treats them as spooky campfire stories. No gore rites. No eye signs.

1. **Noxara** — Night roads, hidden doors, and secret clubs. Thieves may toast her. She is about slipping by, not about cruelty.
2. **Ferox** — The change of shape and the wild run. Some werewolf packs honor Ferox. Other packs refuse any god and call the change a family duty only.
3. **Longiva** — Patience, long nights, and "one more season." Vampire clans toast Longiva. She promises time, not violence.
4. **Ruptor** — Broken oaths, splits, and mutinies. Almost no one keeps a public shrine. When a guild, a legion, a pack, or a bank splits, older folk say Ruptor passed through. This is the lore face of Blake's "factions can splinter" rule.

## Demigods and demigoddesses

Mortal-born, limited shrines, useful as story seeds. They can argue with their parent gods. They can also fall into rival cults.

1. **Caia Wallborn** — Demigoddess of Belloran. The story says she marked the place where the Great Wall should stop, because the mountains would do the rest.
2. **Orin Salt-Hand** — Demigod of Marisca. Taught the first harbor crane and the patience of tides.
3. **Lira of the Quiet Purse** — Demigoddess of Numera. Wrote the first fair-interest rule, so compound interest has a legend that is about fairness, not greed-shaming.
4. **Tam the Night-Gardener** — Child of Terrasa and a dreamer of Somnara. Night-blooming plants (farming plus dreams).
5. **Rexel the Plate-Weld** — Demigod of Ferron. First to bolt a starship hull with a prayer and a wrench, a living emblem of the magic-and-science rivalry that does not pick a winner.

More demigods may be added when Blake's notebooks arrive. Do not add a long list just to fill a page.

## The night tithe (how vampires work, kid-safe)

Vampires in this world are long-lived people with old houses and old manners. They need a little **warmth of the living** to stay well. In play this is the **night tithe**:

- A willing person can sign a contract, sleep a long sleep, and wake tired but unharmed.
- An unwilling tithe is a crime in towns that limit or ban vampires.
- We never show blood, fangs in flesh, or corpses. A hunt is a chase, a rumor, or a missing-person mystery (as with the tax collector quest). People may be "pale" or "worn out," not wounded on screen.
- Kid mode uses the contract and the tired-morning story only.

This is spooky. It is not gory. It also lets banking and vampirism share a language: debts, contracts, interest, and time.

## Vampire clans

All original. They duke it out with each other and with the banking clans.

1. **House Vesperine** — The oldest polite house. Ties to a high-class tavern and rumors at a governor's mansion (the Port Paradise hook already in the plan). Rival of the Aureate Compact. They talk like old money because they *are* old money: centuries of compound interest.
2. **The Pale Ledger** — They treat debts and night tithes like a bank book. Rival of every human bank, especially the Harbor Counting House. Other vampires sometimes call them tacky, which is a clan split waiting to happen.
3. **The Saltwake Kin** — Harbor vampires, night ships, and smuggling. Rival of House Meridian, because they want the same routes.
4. **The Highpass-Shy** — They will not cross the high mountain passes. They live in the low hills below the Wall. Uneasy truce with the Walltrust. They fear the Ridgeward packs they almost never meet, and the stories both sides tell are bigger than the meetings.

Clans can splinter. A junior line can take a new house name and keep a grudge for a hundred years.

## Banking clans (human houses, heirs of old kingdoms)

1. **The Aureate Compact** — The oldest human bank. Claims descent from a fallen kingdom. Motto: "The house remains." Direct rival of House Vesperine. This is the main old-money fight.
2. **House Meridian** — New money, neon towers, starship shares. Rival of the Saltwake Kin, and often of the Aureate Compact, who call them upstarts.
3. **The Walltrust** — Funded the Great Wall and still holds those old bonds. Its fight is with anyone who wants the Wall opened, including some merchants and the Highpass-Shy.
4. **The Harbor Counting House** — Port Paradise's local bank, small and proud. Players make deposits and take loans here in Phase 2. Rival of the Pale Ledger, which would love to buy it.

Banking clans can splinter too: a junior partner, a left-out heir, a regional branch that stops sending books home.

## Werewolf packs

Werewolves live in the wilderness. The change is a moonlit run, fur and howls, a spooky woods story. No gore, no eating people on screen. A fight is a chase or a stand-off. Kid mode can treat a change as "the wolf-shape comes on," then the person wakes in the woods at dawn.

1. **The Ashpine Pack** — Deep forest. Keep to themselves. Many honor Ferox.
2. **The Tidehowl Pack** — Coastal moors near the Harbor Reach. Sometimes they help fishers find a lost child. Sometimes they scare a camp. Ashpine and Tidehowl argue over hunting grounds.
3. **The Ridgeward Pack** — They live in the mountains vampires avoid. Some humans call them the Wall's unpaid watch. They are the natural counterweight to the Highpass-Shy.

Packs splinter when young wolves want to live in town, or when two leaders both claim a valley.
