## The idea in one page

You already have Port Paradise. A new town is not a copy of the whole game. It is:

1. Money and backers, so you can afford a start.
2. A person you send (or go as yourself), with settlers, a chest of coins, and a short charter.
3. A camp that can grow: camp, village, town, city, then kingdom.
4. The same basic layout as Port Paradise, dressed in another culture's buildings.

Talking to that town day to day is not a politics sim. That is the courier and governor-report system in `courier-and-reports-v0.1.md`.

## Phase-first version

Split across phases on purpose, so the skeleton does not swell.

### Phase 2 — make money (no new town yet)

You cannot found a town in this phase. You can start a chest that will matter later.

**Ways to raise a founding chest:**

1. **Your own savings.** Deposits in the Harbor Counting House, with simple compound interest on the world clock (already in the plan). Kid mode shows a savings jar.
2. **A simple bank loan.** You borrow a small, readable amount. Interest compounds per **season**, not per hour. Example draft rate: 5 percent a season on what you still owe. The bank is a character in the drama: it can call in the debt (Phase 2 already named "the bank calling in debts").
3. **A promise of backing** is only a note in this phase (a receipt that says a guild or a house is interested). The real backing is Phase 4 or 5.

Shown balances always skip the forbidden digit run, same as other money screens.

**Compound interest, kept small:**

- Interest is applied on season change, once, on the current balance or the current debt.
- Use small percents and whole coins after rounding.
- Vampire-owned accounts can still grow "for a long time" because those characters live long (already in the plan). That is a story fact, not a second math engine.

### Phase 3 — one buildable lot

Already in the plan: one lot in or just outside Port Paradise, shack to castle, Survival costs materials, Creative is free. This lot can later be **reused as the first camp site** when founding unlocks. Do not add a "Found Town" button here.

### Phase 4 — backers and a paper promise

- A guild, a banking clan, or an investor can **back** you: they put coin in a founding chest in return for a hall, a share, or a law they like.
- This is one quest-like deal with plain choices, not a stock market.
- The deal is drama: if you break it later, they become a rival, or their junior members splinter off and back someone else.

### Phase 5 — actually found the town (the first full version)

**How you send someone:**

1. Pick a **founder**. It can be the character you are playing, or any playable NPC (Blake's switch-character rule).
2. Give them a **founding pack**:
   - the founder
   - a small settler list (Phase-first cap: 4 settlers; later up to 8)
   - a money chest
   - tools and a little grain
   - a **charter** (one page, four choices)
3. Choose a **site** from a short list of claimed plots in a region (see the trade doc). In Creative mode the plot is free. In Survival it costs the chest and the materials.
4. They leave by cart or by boat. A travel timer runs. When they arrive, the camp exists.

**The charter (Phase-first, four lines only):**

- **Who rules:** council, governor (Praefectus), or the founder stays in charge for now. (King, guild rule, and open voting wait for the later constitution page already in the plan.)
- **Taxes:** low, medium, or high.
- **Vampires:** welcome, limited, or banned.
- **Trade:** open, taxed, or tight.

That is enough to start. The longer constitution (magic law, how leaders change, and so on) is the later version of the same page.

**How a new town grows:**

| Step | What you need (draft, Survival) | What unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| Camp | founder arrives with the pack | tents, a fire, a courier post |
| Village | enough food and a few shacks, a season or two | a market board, an Aedile or Duumvir |
| Town | a hall, a counting desk, a wall or dock | a Praefectus, one trade route back to Port Paradise |
| City | two or more districts, a temple or guild hall | Consul and Praetor, a legion cohort may visit |
| Kingdom | more than one town under the same charter | Proconsul, diplomacy, succession drama |

Far-away towns use the cheap daily or seasonal dice roll from the scope lock. They do not run a full NPC life when you are not there. You hear about them by courier.

## Later version (still Phase 5, after the first founded town works)

- More than one founded town, each in a different cultural building style.
- Full constitution page, changeable through the government it set up.
- Investors hold shares. A banking clan and a vampire clan can both own a piece of the same dock, which will cause a fight.
- NPC-founded towns, rare events, not a constant sim (already locked in the plan).
- You can switch to the founder, a settler, a rival governor, or a smuggler on the road (already promised).

### Layout template

Later towns **copy Port Paradise's skeleton**, then dress it:

- a dock or gate (arrival)
- a market (spawn and trade board)
- a high meeting place and a low meeting place (tavern and shack)
- a hall (governor, council, or clan)
- one old relic or landmark (the monolith slot)
- work yards (cranes, forges, or the local craft)
- a counting house or shrine to Numera

**Style swaps** (already named by Blake; Phase 3 can paint one lot, Phase 5 applies a style to a whole town): Middle Eastern, pirate, Scandinavian Viking, Mongolian, Aztec, Inca, and Indigenous American-inspired building styles. Use **original** town names and patterns. Do not copy a real nation's sacred designs. A sensitivity pass happens before any of those styles ship.

## Rivalries and splinters

- Two backers may both want the hall. The founder has to pick, and the loser can fund a rival camp.
- Settlers can split off and start a second camp if loyalty is low (rare, later, same cap as NPC-founded towns).
- A bank can call in the founding loan and try to take the charter.
- If the charter bans vampires, House Vesperine or the Pale Ledger may blockade a route. If it welcomes them, a human militia rumor can start.
